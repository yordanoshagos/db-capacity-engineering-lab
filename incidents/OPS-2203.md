# OPS-2203 — Bed admissions fail with database errors during the mass-casualty drill

| Field        | Value                                    |
|--------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Priority** | P1 — Critical                            |
| **Status**   | Open                                     |
| **Reported by** | ED Operations Lead                    |
| **Component** | `admissions`                            |
| **Affected endpoint** | `POST /api/hospitals/:id/admit` |

## Report (in the reporter's words)

> "During the mass-casualty drill we were admitting a flood of patients to the
> **same hospital** all at once. Admissions started crawling and then a chunk of
> them **failed outright with a database error**. When we admitted to a
> *different* hospital at the same time, that seemed to hold up better. And if we
> just admit one patient at a time, it's completely fine. It only falls apart
> when lots of admissions hit the *same* facility together — which is exactly
> what happens in a real surge."

## What we know

- One-at-a-time admissions succeed.
- Failure appears only under many concurrent admissions to the **same** hospital
  row.
- Different hospitals are less affected by each other.
- Under concurrency the admissions fail — but *how*? Depending on how the system
  is tuned it may surface as a database error, a timeout, or the endpoint simply
  grinding to a standstill. Capture the actual failure signature you observe.

> ⚠️ **This is a field hypothesis, not a verdict.** The suspected cause and
> symptoms above come from whoever filed the ticket — they may be incomplete or
> simply wrong. Your job is to find what is *actually* happening. Disproving the
> reported cause with evidence is a full-marks answer, not a failure.

## Your task

1. Baseline the system (`load-tests/00-baseline.js`).
2. Reproduce the surge: `k6 run load-tests/reproduce-OPS-2203.js`.
3. While it runs, inspect what the database is doing with concurrent writers to
   one row. Characterize the failure mode (a database error? a timeout? a
   collapse to near-zero throughput?) and the mechanism behind it. Work out the
   maximum realistic throughput for a single hospital and why more concurrency
   doesn't raise it.
4. Propose and apply a fix, then re-run and compare.
5. Document everything in `LAB_JOURNAL.md` (Investigation OPS-2203).

_Hint on where to look, not what you'll find: the database exposes who is
waiting on whom, and for how long, while a transaction is in flight._
